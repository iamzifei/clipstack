01 · What it is

One clipboard slot is not enough.

Copy a link, then copy an email address, and the link is gone. ClipStack is a small app in your menu bar that saves every copy as it happens, so nothing you copied is ever lost — and any of it is one shortcut away.

03 · How to use it

Three keys. That's the whole app.

Download, drag ClipStack to Applications, open it. It lives in the menu bar — no Dock icon, no window to manage. Then:

01

Copy, as you always do.

⌘+C

Nothing new to learn. Every time you copy, ClipStack saves it quietly in the background.

02

Press ⇧⌘V to see everything.

⇧+⌘+V

The panel opens over the app you're in, newest first. Use ↑ ↓ or just type to search — the right side shows the whole entry.

03

Press ↩, then paste.

↩→⌘+V

The entry goes back on your clipboard, with its original formatting. Turn on “Paste into the previous app after choosing” in Settings and ↩ pastes it for you.

For the keyboard-only crowd

Hold ⇧⌘. Tap V. Let go.

Like ⌘Tab, but for your clipboard. Keep ⇧⌘ held and tap V again to step back through history; release to paste the highlighted entry. A single press still just opens the panel.

Small moves that add up.

Everything happens inside the same panel, from the keyboard. These are all in the app today.

01

Every entry is tagged.

URL, EMAIL, CODE, COLOR, TEXT, IMAGE, FILE — so you can spot the right one at a glance. Press Tab to show one type at a time; colours get a swatch and HEX, RGB and HSL.

02

Type to find it.

The search field is ready the moment the panel opens. Input methods such as Chinese pinyin work too.

03

Change it before you paste.

⌘K opens transforms for the selected entry: change case, trim, join lines, format or minify JSON, URL-encode, convert a colour, pull the text out of a screenshot (on-device OCR), copy file paths. The result is saved as a new entry.

⌘K
04

Merge several into one paste.

Mark entries with ⌘M (or ⌘-click), then press ↩: they're copied as one, joined by new lines, in the order you marked them.

⌘M↩
05

⌘P keeps an entry at the top of the list for good — your address, a canned reply, a command you run every day. Pinned entries never age out.

⌘O pops any entry out as a small always-on-top note you can drag anywhere. Or press ⌘T and the whole panel stays on top while you type in another app.

06 · Privacy

Your clipboard stays yours.

On-device

Nothing leaves your Mac

History is a plain folder in your Library. No account, no sync, no analytics — the only network request is the update check.

Passwords

Secrets are skipped

Anything a password manager marks as concealed or transient is never recorded (the nspasteboard.org convention).

Permissions

None, out of the box

ClipStack asks for nothing to do its job. Only the two optional paste helpers use Accessibility, and only once you switch one on.

07 · Questions

Everything you might ask.

01What exactly is ClipStack?

A clipboard manager for macOS. It sits in the menu bar and saves everything you copy — text, links, code, images, files — so you can bring any of it back later with ⇧⌘V instead of finding and copying it again.

02Is it really free?

Yes. ClipStack is free and open source under the MIT licence, with no subscription and no paid tier. If it saves you time, there's an optional Ko-fi tip jar.

03How do I install it?

Download the DMG from the releases page, drag ClipStack to Applications and open it. A short welcome guide shows the keys the first time. After that, ClipStack checks for updates and installs them from inside the app.

04Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 13 Ventura or later, on Apple silicon (M-series) Macs.

05Is my clipboard data private?

Everything stays on your Mac, in ~/Library/Application Support/ClipStack. Nothing is uploaded, and entries a password manager marks as concealed are never saved. You can delete one entry with ⌘⌫ or clear everything from the menu-bar menu.

06Does it need any permissions?

Not to work. Two optional settings — paste straight into the app you came from, and hold ⇧⌘ to cycle — need macOS Accessibility, and macOS only asks when you turn one on.

07Can I change the shortcuts?

Yes. In Settings, click any shortcut — open the panel, open Settings, clear history, or the hold-to-cycle combo — and press the new keys.

08What languages does it speak?

English, 简体中文, 繁體中文, 日本語, 한국어, Español, Français and Deutsch. It follows your Mac's language, or pick one in Settings.
